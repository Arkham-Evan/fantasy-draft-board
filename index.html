    Monte Carlo draft simulator

    Simulates the full 15-round draft thousands of times. Your nine opponents pick from a randomised ADP order (each player's own draft-position variance), and your picks are chosen by a randomised greedy policy on value over replacement. Every simulated roster is scored on its best legal starting lineup, then the best-performing drafts are summarised into a plan.

    The biggest edge: wait on QB

    The market takes Josh Allen at 24.8 and Drake Maye at 46.2. But Jayden Daniels sits at 71.7 and Jalen Hurts at 74.9 — both healthy, both with top-five upside and elite rushing floors.

    In a 10-team 1QB league the gap between QB3 and QB10 is a few points a week. Taking Daniels or Hurts in Round 7 instead of Allen in Round 3 buys you an extra elite RB or WR at almost no positional cost. Lamar Jackson at 50.9 is the middle-ground play if you want more certainty.

    Second edge: both elite TEs are cheap

    Trey McBride (44.0) and Brock Bowers (44.8) are healthy with no injury tags, at a Round 5 price. That's a discount to their talent. Take one, then ignore the position.

    Plan by draft slot

    Picks 1–3

    Gibbs, Bijan, or Chase — no thought. Long wait to your second pick, so plan for Jefferson (if he lasts past 15) plus an elite TE or RB at the 2/3 turn. QB waits until Round 7.

    Picks 4–7

    Most flexible slot. JSN, Taylor, or London. Best-player-available through Round 4, then McBride/Bowers in Round 5 and Daniels/Hurts in Round 7.

    Picks 8–10

    Achane or London should fall to you. Use the back-to-back picks to double-tap RB at the 2/3 turn (Chase Brown, Hampton, Love, Walker), then flood WR in Rounds 4–6 where Higgins, Irving, and Brian Thomas Jr. are all discounted.

    Rounds 13–15: kicker, defense, fliers

    Kicker — Round 14, not earlier

    Brandon Aubrey (DAL, ADP 131) is the only kicker worth a deliberate pick. Otherwise Ka'imi Fairbairn (HOU, 130) or Cam Little (JAX, 165). Kicker scoring is close to random; the only real edge is offensive quality.

    Defense — Round 15, then stream

    Denver (91) and Seattle (99) go far too early. Take LAR (131), NE (132), or PHI (140) and stream weekly off the schedule. Never carry two.

    What to do with the true last picks

    Two RB handcuffs to workhorse backs (Jordan Mason, Blake Corum, Kyle Monangai), one WR attached to a high-volume passing game, and Travis Kelce at 128 as a free-roll TE2. Everything else at this depth belongs on the Week 2 waiver wire.
